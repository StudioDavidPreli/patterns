01Overview

What it does

Block & Line draws patterns that look like they were printed and not rendered.

A grid of colored rectangles sits on a paper-grain background. Ink stripes overprint on top, sometimes black, sometimes white-on-dark, sometimes interrupted by the line breakage and jitter knobs. Two seeds control the result: one picks the geometry, one picks the colors. Sliders control everything in between.

The interesting part of the build was a bug. The two seeds were not independent. Clicking "New colors" appeared to reshape the layout. Clicking "New layout" appeared to reshuffle the palette. The rectangles held position. Everything inside them moved. The fix moved per-cell decisions out of the render pass and into the layout pass, where they belonged, and the seeds decoupled cleanly.

The rest of the build is extension work: per-cell variance on stroke spacing and weight, a Latin binomial pinned to the two seeds, a "Randomize all" button at the top of the sidebar, four animation toggles, and a Pause button that lets the user freeze on a generation they like.

02The interface

Sidebar at 252 pixels, canvas on the right

The page splits left to right. Sidebar at 252 pixels wide on the left. Canvas on the right. Paper grain showing through everything.

The sidebar runs five sections, top to bottom, with a "Randomize all" button pinned at the very top:

  • Composition. Aspect ratio. Color granularity, the minimum cell size for the color subdivision. Color split bias, horizontal versus vertical. The same two sliders for the stroke subdivision. A "Share layouts" toggle that ties the two subdivisions together.
  • Color layer. Fill density and black dominance. The black slider is doing more work than its name suggests: at 87% the canvas reads as black ink with red and yellow accents, which is the loaded default.
  • Stroke layer. Fill density. Horizontal share. Spacing and weight, with per-cell variance for both. White-on-dark share for the stripe color.
  • Texture. Paper grain, line jitter, line breakage. The breakage slider on the loaded default is at 80%, which gives the stripes a worried, photocopied quality.
  • Animation. Toggles for layout, colors, ratio, and randomization. A tick interval slider, and a Pause button that clears every animation toggle in one click.

At the bottom: "New layout," "New colors," "Save PNG," "Save SVG."

03Architecture

Two passes on a p5.js canvas

The pattern is rendered in two passes on a p5.js canvas.

layoutSeed → subdivide() → colorCells, strokeCells (rectangles)
              ↓
              assignCellStructure(): per-cell booleans + rolls

renderSeed → pickColor(): palette picks per filled cell
layoutSeed-derived constant → line jitter, line breakage

subdivide()

subdivide() is a recursive splitter. At each level it chooses between a horizontal cut and a vertical cut, biased by the user's split-bias slider and modulated by the cell's current aspect. It recurses into each child until a depth-and-area stop condition fires. The minimum cell size acts as a hard floor.

assignCellStructure()

assignCellStructure() runs immediately after subdivision. It walks each cell and writes:

  • c.filled and c.strokeFilled: should this cell get drawn at all
  • c.horizontal: do the stripes run sideways
  • c.whiteOnDark: are the stripes white instead of black
  • c.spacingRoll and c.weightRoll: raw [0,1] values for the per-cell stroke variance

These properties are tied to layoutSeed. Once they're recorded, renderSeed controls only the palette picks inside pickColor(), and a layoutSeed-derived constant seeds the line jitter and breakage.

04The bug

Two buttons, one effect

The first complaint was obvious in thirty seconds of clicking around. "New colors" was not just changing colors. It was changing where the stripes ran, which cells were empty, whether the ink was black or white. The rectangles held position. Everything inside them flipped. "New layout" had the symmetric problem: rerolling the geometry also rerolled the palette.

The diagnosis lived in the draw loop. After the layout subdivision finished, the draw pass reseeded with renderSeed and walked both cell lists, calling p.random() in order to decide:

  1. whether the color cell got filled at all
  2. which palette color it received
  3. whether the stripe cell got rendered
  4. whether the stripes ran horizontal or vertical
  5. whether the stripes were white instead of black

Five decisions, one stream, one seed. The boundary between "layout" and "render" was a label on the buttons but not a property of the code.

Two failure modes, inverted:

Click "New layout"

The rectangles change. renderSeed is untouched, so the random stream that drives pickColor() is the same sequence as before. But the cells it lands on are different. The colors look new. The user reads this as "my palette changed when I asked for new geometry."

Click "New colors"

The rectangles do not change. The cells in colorCells and strokeCells are the same arrays of the same rectangles. But the same five-deep stream of decisions per cell now runs against a new seed, so cells flip from filled to empty, stripes flip horizontal to vertical, the white ink moves around. The user reads this as "my layout changed when I asked for new colors." It did not, technically. The cells were unchanged. What changed was every render-time decision about what to put inside them, which is most of what the eye reads as layout.

The bug was not that the seeds were entangled in any deep way. The seed boundary had been drawn around geometry only, when the user was reading it around the visible result.

05The fix

Move the decisions to where they belonged

A new function, assignCellStructure(), runs immediately after the cells are subdivided. It seeds a dedicated random stream off layoutSeed and walks each cell once, writing the four booleans listed above plus the two stroke rolls.

After the fix, the draw pass reseeds with renderSeed only for the palette pick inside pickColor(). Textural decisions get their own reseed off a layoutSeed-derived constant, so palette swaps no longer disturb the line texture.

Restructure without rebuild

A subtlety. The four threshold sliders (colorFill, strokeFill, strokeH, whiteOnDark) need to respect the new seeded stream, but they cannot trigger a full geometry rebuild every time the user nudges a value. The sidebar would feel like it was fighting back. So assignCellStructure() got separated from regenerateLayout(), and those four sliders carry a restructure: true flag that re-runs only the per-cell decisions. The cell rectangles stay put.

Because the dedicated stream resets to the same seed before every restructure pass, raising a threshold only flips cells whose random roll just crossed the new value. A cell filled at colorFillProb = 0.5 stays filled at 0.6. The slider feels live and stable, both.

06Stroke variance

Per-cell randomization on spacing and weight

The next request was per-cell randomization on stroke spacing and weight. The implementation choice: store the raw random() value on each cell as c.spacingRoll and c.weightRoll in [0,1], then compute final values at draw time:

const sMul = 1 + (c.spacingRoll * 2 - 1) * 0.7 * params.strokeSpacingVar;
const wMul = 1 + (c.weightRoll  * 2 - 1) * 0.7 * params.strokeWeightVar;

Variance at zero collapses both to the base. Variance at one ranges over base × [0.3, 1.7]. The base sliders and the variance sliders all stay fully live, without re-rolling per-cell structure on every drag. The cell that was thickest stays the thickest. Only the magnitudes change.

A side effect: when a cell's weight crosses its spacing, the stripes overprint into a solid block. Not clamped. Some compositions benefit from the occasional black square in a field of stripes.

07Specimen names

A binomial driven by the two seeds

The static specimen m. label was replaced with a Latin binomial driven by the two seeds. The genus is drawn from a fixed pool of forty Latin nouns by layoutSeed % 40. The species is drawn from a fixed pool of forty Latin adjectives by renderSeed % 40.

const POOL_GENUS   = ['Tessella','Tessera','Stratum', ... ];
const POOL_SPECIES = ['nigrata','albata','picta',     ... ];

Modulo, not hash. The mapping is intentionally observable. A user who notices that Tessella recurs every forty layouts has correctly read the system. Same first word, same layoutSeed mod 40. Same second word, same renderSeed mod 40.

No Latin grammatical agreement between genus and species. Linea nigrofracta is not classically correct. Granum discolor is shaky. The binomial is a tag, not a taxonomy.

08Randomize all

One button at the top of the sidebar

A "Randomize all" button at the top of the sidebar walks every slider in the registry. Picks a uniform value snapped to that input's step. Applies it. Flips the share-layouts toggle 50/50. Rerolls both seeds. One button, every parameter, every roll.

Aspect ratio is the one slider it does not touch. A canvas that resizes on every reroll is more disruptive than playful, so aspect is opt-in via its own animation toggle below.

09Animation

Four toggles on the same tick

Four toggles in the Animation section, each ticking on the same setTimeout loop:

  • Animate layout. Increments layoutSeed by one each tick.
  • Animate colors. Increments renderSeed by one each tick.
  • Animate ratio. Steps the aspect slider one notch each tick, wrapping at max back to min.
  • Animate randomization. Calls the randomize-all path each tick. Subsumes the per-seed paths since both seeds are rerolled anyway.

The toggles compose. Layout + colors gives a slow drift through all combinations of the binomial. Ratio + colors morphs the canvas shape while the palette rotates. Animate randomization on its own is full-strobe parameter chaos, useful for finding starting points.

A Pause button under the tick interval clears all four toggles and stops the timer in one click. Whatever frame is currently on screen stays put. The user can then save the PNG or SVG of that exact specimen.

Black-dominant, sparse stripes, thick weight, heavy breakage. The starting screen reads as something between a worn poster and a magnified halftone. The user is meant to see what the tool wants to make first, then push it elsewhere.

Click "New colors" twenty times. The genus holds. The species rotates: nigrata, albata, picta, variegata, maculata. The rectangles do not move.
