The species column uses the project's vocabulary (see `SPECIES.md`). The render column is an approximate name for the visible breed, not a binding identification.

## New species suggested by the reference

Species identified from the source set that don't map to the original taxonomy:

**cylinder** (SHAPE-78, 113). Built. 3D cylindrical tube. See `species/specimen_cylinder_v2.html`.

**bar** (SHAPE-115). A 3D rod or pair of rods. Solid, oriented, slender. Could be implemented as a stretched `THREE.BoxGeometry` or a capsule.

**anemone** (SHAPE-05, 09, 58). Not yet built. Recurring form across three references. Irregular cellular/organic marks. Identified during cluster reclassification (2026-05-18).

**agglomerate** (SHAPE-25, 80). Not yet built. Irregular spheres clustered together. Identified during cluster reclassification (2026-05-18).

**basalt** (SHAPE-82). Not yet built. Networked cubes. Identified during cluster reclassification (2026-05-18).

**pinwheel** (SHAPE-117). Not yet built. Irregular spoke-and-hub form. Identified during cluster reclassification (2026-05-18).

## New breeds suggested by the reference

Breeds identified from the cluster reclassification (2026-05-18):

**color cluster**. Scattered colored marks within a silhouette. Variable size, color per mark. The dominant breed across former cluster-classified shapes (05, 09, 16, 25, 32, 58, 80, 97, 107, 113, 117, 130). 12 reference shapes.

**dot matrix**. Equal-size marks at uniform opacity, placement noise-driven or grid-based. Takes a glyph parameter: dot (27, 43, 88) or block (21). Also appears sparse (136). 5 reference shapes.

**mycelium** (SHAPE-77). Network/line-based connections. Seen on a torus form. 1 reference shape.

**blues** (SHAPE-82). Light and shadow renderer with static noise introducing negative space in the blue region. 1 reference shape.

## Notes on ambiguity

A handful of shapes sit between two species. My calls in the table reflect best guesses; reasonable people might classify differently.

- SHAPE-22, 106 are blocks with eye decorations layered on top. Currently classified as block; could be classified as eye with a block backdrop. The composition question (multiple species in one rendering) doesn't have a clean resolution at the single-species level.
- SHAPE-34, 94 are starburst/splat shapes between star and blob. Classified as blob because the silhouette is irregular rather than polygonal.
- SHAPE-44, 50, 143 are flat ovals that could be disc, torus from a thin angle, or ring. Classified by visual emphasis (depth or no depth).
- SHAPE-67 is a halftone field with a white oval cut out. Classified as disc with surround treatment; could equally be block with a disc hole.
- SHAPE-87 reads as a block silhouette filled with raster, but the silhouette is generated by the raster itself, not an underlying form. Borderline case for "is the species the silhouette or the marks?"
- SHAPE-105, 141 are between bolt and filament. Bolt suggests sharp angular pieces; filament suggests continuous curve. Both apply at different scales here.

## What this tells us about priorities

Looking at frequency, the top six species in the reference (blob, torus, filament, ring, radial, block) cover 111 of 144 shapes, roughly 77%. With cluster dissolved into a breed, the coverage of built species is higher than originally estimated.

Of those six, blob and filament are both 2D-native and structurally similar (one closed, one open). Torus and ring share an implementation path (torus is the 3D version of ring). Radial is mostly lines from a center. All six are built or planned.

The color cluster breed (12 reference shapes) and dot matrix breed (5 reference shapes) are the highest-value new breeds to build. They unlock visual coverage across many existing species without requiring new species implementations.

Practically: filament is still the right next species. After that, blob is the natural follow-up because it tests a different 2D-native primitive (closed silhouette vs open curve) and unlocks the largest single chunk of the reference set.

## Render style approximations

The render style column in the master table is rough. It uses a vocabulary that overlaps but doesn't exactly match the project's breed names:

- "raster vertical," "raster" — corresponds to the built `raster_vertical` breed
- "stipple," "color stipple" — corresponds to `stipple` (color stipple uses tinted ink, not yet a feature)
- "riso color," "color blocking" — corresponds to `riso_noise` and `color_blocking`
- "outline" — planned breed
- "solid fill" — planned breed
- "halftone," "halftone surround," "halftone color" — planned breed in different configurations
- "mosaic" — planned breed
- "dot ring," "color cluster," "mark cluster," "sparse dots" — these describe stipple-family variations
- "starburst," "branching," "wave" — these describe filament/radial-family compositions

These render notes are useful for planning which breeds need building to reproduce a given reference shape. They are not binding identifications.
