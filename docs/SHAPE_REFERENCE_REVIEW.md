# Shape reference: independent review

My own reading of the 144 reference shapes from the ogRef images. Written without consulting the existing SHAPE_REFERENCE.md. Classifications use the project's species and breed vocabulary where possible.

Reviewed 2026-05-13 from E0000_06.jpg through E0000_011.jpg.

## Observations on the reference set as a whole

Before the per-shape table, some patterns that jump out:

**Negative space is everywhere.** Many shapes are rendered as white forms on black/dark backgrounds, or as cutouts in dense stipple/halftone fields. This is not a rare variant. It appears across species: discs, blobs, blocks, rings, stars, bolts. Negative-space rendering is a first-class rendering mode in the reference, not an edge case.

**Background fields are load-bearing.** Shapes 12, 15, 20, 31, 40, 52, 60, 66, 67, 69, 84, 101, 119, 139 all have the background doing significant visual work. The background is not just "paper" -- it is a dense stipple, halftone grid, or solid black that the form punches through. The breed system currently assumes the breed draws *into* the silhouette. The reference set frequently inverts this.

**Color is a breed concern, not a species concern.** The same species appears in monochrome, duotone, and full multicolor across different shapes. Color palette is part of the rendering treatment, not the form.

**3D species are a minority.** Most shapes are flat/2D in character, even when they have shading. The 3D-rendered torus, arch, block, and cylinder shapes are distinct in the set but represent maybe 20-25% of the total.

**Many shapes sit on a spectrum between two species.** The boundary between blob and disc, ring and torus, cluster and stipple-breed, radial and star is genuinely fuzzy in places. I've made calls but noted the ambiguities.

## Disagreements and difficult calls

### SHAPE-01: bristle vs radial
I classified this as bristle because the arms are irregular, knotted, and organic. The existing taxonomy would probably call it radial (lines from center) or cluster (scattered marks). But the character is distinctly bristle-like: a central mass with irregular spiky projections. The yellow accent dots at the tips reinforce the biological/organic reading.

### SHAPE-03: torus vs blob
Classified as torus because the form is a ring with interior visible, not a solid oval. The oval boundary has a hole. But the rendering is loose enough that it could be read as an oval blob with a lighter center.

### SHAPE-06: breed bleeding past silhouette
The raster lines extend past the dome form at the top. This is the only shape in the set where the breed rendering clearly exceeds the silhouette boundary. Worth noting as a breed behavior, not a species behavior.

### SHAPE-12: disc vs ring vs concentric
This is complicated. The dark stipple field is the background. The white circular area is the disc cutout. But inside that disc, there appears to be a concentric ring or dot structure. This could be: a disc with an inner element (concentric disc), a ring in negative space (the white area is the ring body), or a disc with a separate central mark layered on top. I classified as disc but the inner structure suggests the disc species needs more than just a filled ellipse.

### SHAPE-34: radial vs blob
I went with radial because the arms are distinct lines from a center, not an amorphous boundary. The existing doc calls this blob (splat). It sits exactly on the boundary. The question is whether the silhouette is the defining feature (blob: amorphous boundary) or the internal structure (radial: lines from center).

### SHAPE-44: blob vs torus
Blue asymmetric teardrop. Could be a torus seen from a specific angle where depth makes one side wider. I went with blob because the shape is asymmetric and solid, not ring-like.

### SHAPE-63: ring vs torus vs chain
Two interlocking oval loops in pink, green, orange. This doesn't fit any single species cleanly. It's a chain-link form. Could be a new species, or a specialized ring/torus variant. Classified as ring but this is a stretch.

### SHAPE-78: blob vs cylinder
The existing doc calls this cylinder (3D tube). I see a colorful egg/teardrop form with organic marks. It doesn't read as cylindrical to me. The depth cues are ambiguous. Classified as blob.

### SHAPE-82: cluster vs blob
Blue chunky interlocking shapes. Reads as a cluster because the shapes are distinct separate marks, not a continuous silhouette. But the marks are arranged in a tight gear-like configuration that could be a single complex blob.

### SHAPE-105: bolt vs bristle
Black spiky angular marks. The existing doc calls this bolt. But the marks are not a single zigzag line; they're a cluster of sharp angular fragments. Could be bolt fragments, bristle spikes without a body, or a cluster of angular marks.

### SHAPE-141: filament vs bolt
I classified as filament because the line is thin, continuous, and wavy rather than sharply angular. But it has some zigzag quality. The existing project used SHAPE-141 as the bristle reference (from a different interpretation). In the actual image, it reads as a thin wandering colored line.

## Structural observations

### Species that need internal structure
Several shapes show that species are not just silhouettes with uniform interiors:
- **Disc** needs an inner radius (12, 13, 67, 139 all show annulus/ring-like disc forms)
- **Block** needs holes (45, 89, 134)
- **Eye** is defined by its internal concentric structure (36, 99)
- **Concentric** is entirely about internal ring structure (49, 86, 137, 144)

### Negative space as a cross-cutting concern
Negative polarity appears in every species except concentric and bar. It's not species-specific. It behaves like a rendering mode (invert the silhouette: draw outside, leave inside empty) that applies to any species/breed combination.

### Background field types in negative-space shapes
When a shape uses negative polarity, the background is one of:
- Solid black (most common: 4, 8, 13, 15, 22, 25, 29, 30, 41, 54, 57, 79, 84, 99, 103, 106, 111, 126, 129, 142)
- Dense stipple/noise (12, 31, 40, 60, 67, 119, 139)
- Halftone grid (52, 66, 69, 92, 101)

The background field type is itself a breed-level choice (what render style fills the surround). This suggests negative-space rendering interacts with breeds: the "negative stipple" breed would fill the canvas with stipple outside the silhouette.
